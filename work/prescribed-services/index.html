Approach

Start with the prescription. Use the wait.

The old flow made people describe their prescription, then upload it. The new one reads it first and lets them book during approval.

Before · form first

9 steps, one dead wait
Pick patient→Pick services→Pick tests→Pick benefit→Upload Rx→Agent types it in→Wait ~20 min→Come back→Pay

After · prescription first

AI reads, people decide
Upload Rx→AI reads it · 5–10 s→Confirm details→Submit details→Book a preferred slot→Item-by-item result→Pay→Confirmed
In parallel

An agent approves each test while the patient picks a slot, so the ~7 minutes do useful work.

Product

Four moments that changed the outcome

The screens where the key decisions live.

01 · UPLOAD AND READ

Make the AI's work visible

Parsing takes 5–10 seconds, so the scan and checks show live. Visible progress makes waiting easier.

02 · REVIEW

Show where the AI is unsure

Handwritten prescriptions read at 50–70% confidence. Uncertain tests turn amber, and the patient adds or removes each one.

03 · BOOK

Turn the wait into progress

Picks are preferences, held for 30 minutes and confirmed after approval. If one's gone, the same lab's other slots come first.

04 · RESULT

Approve per test, always with a reason

One uncovered test no longer blocks the rest. Every rejection says why and what to do next.

Working with tech and ops

Their answers reshaped the screens

I took my open questions to tech and ops before finalising. Each answer changed the design.

How long does parsing take?5–10 seconds

Changed: a live scan, so the wait feels like progress.

How confident is the AI?~90% printed, 50–70% handwritten

Changed: uncertain tests turn amber, and the patient adds or removes them.

What if parsing fails?One silent retry, then a manual route

Changed: the first failure stays invisible and nothing is lost.

Can a slot be held for 30 minutes?Not when demand is high

Changed: slots became preferences, confirmed after approval.

What happens with a duplicate?Warn, never block

Changed: a warning the patient can move past.

How long to build Phase 1?At least 1.5 months

Changed: the wait fix shipped first.

Usability testing

27 hallway tests. Three fixes to the upload step.

I ran hallway tests with 27 colleagues and staff members. Each person went through the booking flow while I watched and tracked where they got stuck.

27testers: colleagues and staff members
Hallwayquick, in-person sessions
ModeratedI ran each session and tracked the results
3fixes, all on the upload step
01 · EXPECTATIONS

They expected the old wait

What we sawTesters weren't clear how their prescription gets digitised. Most assumed it would take as long as before.

Changed: the upload screen shows each step and how long it takes, before anyone starts.

02 · GOING BACK

Going back meant a slow reload

What we sawTesters who went back after submitting a prescription waited a long time for the screen to load. Engineering suggested removing the way back.

Changed: a short confirmation before submitting, so the prescription is picked once, on purpose.

03 · VALIDATION RULES

The rules read as fine print

What we sawMost testers took the prescription guidelines for a routine disclaimer and skipped them.

Changed: a visual checklist, marked on a sample, of what a valid prescription must show.
