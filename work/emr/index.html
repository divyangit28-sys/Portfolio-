This is how we helped on-site clinics swap paper registers for one simple record that works for doctors, nurses and the employers who care for them.

Business problem

Three value leaks

02
Problem

Business brief: “We need a proprietary software system for our on-site clinics to stop revenue leakage, and drive wellness sales.”

Stakeholder interviews with sales and operations named three leaks.

37%
of renewal contracts at risk with no proof of health impact
24%
of potential revenue lost to outside pharmacies and labs
90%
of visits are acute care, while about 60% of employees have chronic disorders

Validate each leak with data, find its root cause, and design an EMR that fixes the most valuable one first, for doctors, nurses, patients and the HR teams who renew the contract.

Goal
B2B retention Revenue leakage Chronic care EMR
The challenge 03

No digital health records existed. Every prescription, lab order and diagnosis lived in paper registers, with no searchable history.

With no records to analyse, how do you test a business case?We had three hypotheses to validate and no historical data, so we built the evidence another way:
5,000 Baselines came from a survey sent to 5,000 employees.
Recall Historical patterns came from physician and sales-team memory, treated as directional, not conclusive, and flagged as such.
Insight The data gap that made this research hard is the business problem itself: a clinic that can't answer basic questions about its patients needs this product.

What we asked the sales team, employees and clinic staff

Workflow findings 12

Paper is fast for the doctor, and expensive for everyone after them.

3Fragmented recordsData lives in a register, a prescription pad and the doctor's own ledger. With no history, each consult starts from the patient's memory.
20 minHandover is the sharpest painNurses cross-check every prescription against inventory by hand. Long waits push patients to outside pharmacies.
2 rolesA clean role splitDoctors need clinical inputs: history, vitals, diagnosis, dosage. Nurses need queue, vitals entry, hand-off notes and stock. Patients carry the continuity.
2 minSpeed beats completenessDoctors prescribe in shorthand and repeat chronic meds verbally. Anything with more clicks than paper will be resisted.
NervousHand-offs are informalNotes pass on paper in both directions. Nurses worry about being blamed for a dropped instruction.
StampTrust is physical todayPharmacies need signature, stamp, registration number and date. A digital Rx needs an equivalent, or it won't be honoured.
Trust & adoption 13
Patient, interview
“My medical information will not be available with my HR or manager, right?”

A direct tension between two validated problems: renewals need HR-facing reporting, while patients need their data kept away from the employer. The EMR's data architecture has to resolve both, with aggregate reports only.

Design principles 16
01 Never slow down the clinical moment Anything added to a doctor's or nurse's real-time workflow must be as fast as their paper habit, or faster.
02 Preserve the shorthand Keep the mental models doctors already use, and translate their lingo into plain language for patients.
03 Surface value at the point of decision Show information and options where the choice is made, so value isn't lost to searching.
Speed Familiar shorthand Right info, right moment
