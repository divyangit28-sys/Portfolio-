02 · At a glance

A small screen, a fair disagreement, and an experiment to settle it

Problem

Users dropped off while their prescription was under review, with no explanation of the wait.

Hypothesis

Users who know what is happening, and what comes next, are less likely to abandon.

Constraint

Engineering questioned whether a screen outside the core transaction was worth the build effort.

Result

The context screen and timer cut drop-off by 20% in an A/B test, 24 participants per group.

03 · User friction
Why it matters more here

People ordering medicine often need it soon, and have just shared a medical document. Uncertainty costs more trust than in an ordinary checkout.

Silence after the upload

At the moment of review, the original flow said nothing. Silence after an upload is ambiguous: it can read as a failed upload, a frozen app or a missed step.

A behavioural problem, not a visual one: users dropped off during review.
04 · UX hypothesis

Uncertainty, not the wait itself, drives abandonment

IF
Users understand what is happening to their Rx
AND
They know clearly what happens next
THEN
Fewer will abandon the order during review
Why this hypothesis
Designable
Review takes time. What users know while it happens is ours to shape.
Grounded
Visibility of system status: an explained wait feels shorter.
Falsifiable
It names a behaviour, a cause and a direction, so it can be tested.
05 · Proposed solution

One screen. Each element answers a question.

1
Confirmation the Rx is being processed
Did my upload work? → Reassurance
2
Explanation of the review process
What is happening, and why wait? → Makes the wait feel intentional
3
Timer or estimated waiting state
How long? Has it stalled? → System status and expectations
4
What happens next
Do I need to act? → A reason to stay
The timer was not decoration. It existed to reduce uncertainty and prove the process had not stalled.

08 · Result

Explaining the wait reduced drop-off by 20%

The variant with the timer and context-setting screen outperformed the control. Users who knew what was happening, why, and what came next were more likely to stay through review.

Reading it honestly

With 24 participants per group, this is a directional signal, not a statistically conclusive one. It answered the question we asked: is this experience worth further investment?
